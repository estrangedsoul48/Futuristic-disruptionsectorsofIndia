Part Seven · Agentic AI Security
§07
AI Security · The Next Decade

Agentic AI as the Biggest Security Bottleneck of 2025–2035

The emergence of agentic AI — systems that autonomously plan, act, and operate inside enterprise environments — creates a security architecture problem that existing tools cannot solve. The decade from 2025–2035 will be defined by the race to build trust infrastructure for a world where software agents outnumber human employees.

"By 2028, enterprise AI agents will outnumber human employees 3:1 in technology-intensive companies. Every agent needs an identity, a permission set, an audit trail, and a kill-switch. No enterprise has this infrastructure today."

Agentic AI Security Thesis — Cybersecurity Superwave Research
Pillar 01 · Identity
Non-Human Identity (NHI) Management
Every AI agent, service account, API key, and machine credential needs an identity with lifecycle management and least-privilege enforcement. NHI management is the fastest-growing IAM sub-category, growing 10× faster than human IAM as agent populations explode. CyberArk, Okta, and NHI-native startups (Aembit, Astrix) are competing for this greenfield.
Immediate Priority
Pillar 02 · Authorization
Agent Permission & Scope Control
Agents must operate under fine-grained permission controls that limit their blast radius if compromised. Authorization frameworks for agentic AI — analogous to OAuth 2.0 for humans — are nascent but will become mandatory enterprise infrastructure within 24 months as the first high-profile agent hijacking incidents drive board-level urgency across industries.
12–24 Months
Pillar 03 · Monitoring
Runtime Monitoring & Anomaly Detection
Detecting when agents deviate from expected task sequences, attempt unauthorized resource access, or show signs of compromise requires purpose-built observability tooling. Traditional APM tools are insufficient for AI agents whose "normal" behavior is inherently variable and context-dependent. AI-native behavioral baselines are the new requirement.
Emerging
Pillar 04 · Governance
AI Governance & Compliance Infrastructure
The EU AI Act, US EO on AI, and national AI governance frameworks impose liability for AI systems without explainability and audit trails. AI governance platforms (Credo AI, Holistic AI) translate regulatory requirements into operational controls. The most regulation-certain category in AI security — spend is mandated, not discretionary.
EU AI Act Mandated
Pillar 05 · Trust
Human Verification & Anti-Deepfake Layer
As AI agents proliferate, proving a counterparty is actually human becomes a security prerequisite. Liveness detection, cryptographic attestation of human presence, and digital watermarking become the trust layer of the AI economy — analogous to SSL certificates for the web. Every interaction at deepfake risk requires this infrastructure.
12–24 Months
Pillar 06 · Emergency Controls
Agent Kill-Switches & Circuit Breakers
The ability to instantly pause, quarantine, or terminate a compromised agent across thousands of running instances is existential. Agent orchestration platforms must provide kill-switch infrastructure analogous to circuit breakers in financial trading — automated, instant, and faster than the attack itself. This is an architectural requirement, not a product feature.

Part Ten · Critical Infrastructure
§10
Critical Infrastructure & National Security

Where Cybersecurity Becomes a Mandatory Infrastructure Layer

These six infrastructure domains have crossed the threshold where a successful cyber attack is no longer an IT incident — it is a national security event with potential military-grade consequences and population-scale disruption.

Energy & Power
Grid Security
NERC CIP mandates, nation-state pre-positioning, and physical-cyber convergence make power grid security the highest-consequence domain. A successful grid attack in a major economy is treated as an act of war. Security procurement is mandated by regulators, not CFOs — making spend fully non-discretionary and recession-proof.
Telecommunications
5G Network Security
5G network slicing creates new attack surfaces at the radio access layer. SS7 protocol vulnerabilities still plague global carrier interconnects. Telecom is simultaneously the backbone of digital government, financial infrastructure, and military communications — a compounded single point of failure for multiple civilization-scale services.
Defense Systems
Defense Modernization
Classified networks, supply chain security for defense hardware, and cyber-physical weapons systems create a defense cybersecurity market growing at 2× commercial rates with near-zero price sensitivity. AUKUS, Five Eyes, and NATO interoperability agreements drive sustained allied security spending cycles.
Financial Systems
Payment Rails & Core Banking
SWIFT, real-time payment networks (UPI, FedNow, TIPS), CBDCs, and core banking systems form the nervous system of the global economy. A successful attack on payment infrastructure is immediately visible to every citizen. DORA, FFIEC, and PCI-DSS mandate specific controls making financial sector security among the most regulatory-compelled categories globally.
Space Systems
Satellite & Orbital Security
The 2022 Viasat KA-SAT attack — disabling tens of thousands of terminals across Europe at the start of Russia's Ukraine invasion — demonstrated that satellite communications are frontline military and civilian infrastructure simultaneously. Space security is severely underfunded relative to its strategic importance.
AI Infrastructure
Data Centers & AI Compute
Hyperscale data centers running AI inference workloads are the new critical infrastructure. Physical security, power supply security, and cyber security must converge as nation-state actors target AI compute assets strategically. The concentration of global AI capacity in a handful of facilities creates systemic risk at civilization scale.

§11
India's Cybersecurity Opportunity

Where India Can Build Global Leadership

India occupies a uniquely advantageous position in the global cybersecurity economy: the world's largest pool of English-speaking engineering talent, a government-run digital public infrastructure stack at unprecedented scale, and a 1.4B-person domestic market undergoing rapid simultaneous digitization. The opportunity is large but requires different strategies in each category.

Category 01 · Services · Achievable Now
Managed Security Services (MSSP / MDR)
India delivers 40%+ of global IT services. The combination of deep technical talent, 24/7 operational capacity across time zones, and 50–60% labour cost advantage creates a structurally superior MDR delivery model. TCS, Infosys, Wipro, and HCL already operate security operations centres at scale. This is India's clearest near-term path to global security leadership.
Already Leading
Category 02 · Infrastructure · Already Demonstrated
Digital Identity & Trust Infrastructure
Aadhaar serves 1.4B people — the largest biometric identity system ever built. India has proven the ability to build identity infrastructure at civilization scale. eMudhra as a licensed CA, NPCI operating UPI's identity layer, DigiLocker as a document trust platform — all represent world-class DPI security that other nations are actively studying and attempting to replicate.
Global Benchmark
Category 03 · Defense · Regional Leadership
Defense Cybersecurity & Electronic Warfare
India's defense modernization program, Make in India initiative, and DRDO's expanded mandate create a protected domestic market for defense-grade cybersecurity. BEL, Data Patterns, and KPIT Technologies are building defense electronics security capabilities. The India-China border tension provides ongoing political urgency and non-discretionary government budget commitments.
Building
Category 04 · Telecom · Emerging Markets Leader
Telecom Fraud Analytics & Communication Security
India's telecom market (800M+ mobile subscribers, world's largest UPI volume) creates domestic demand at scale for telecom fraud management. Subex and Tanla Platforms have built world-class capabilities now exported to African, Southeast Asian, and Middle Eastern operators — demonstrating that India can own telecom security globally, not just domestically.
Exporting
Category 05 · AI Security · 5–10 Year Window
AI Governance & Security Research
India's AI talent base — concentrated in IITs, IIITs, and research labs — creates a competitive foundation for AI security research and governance products. The IndiaAI Mission is building domestic AI infrastructure that requires security governance. Indian startups are beginning to emerge in AI red-teaming and compliance tools, though the market remains nascent relative to US peers.
Emerging
Category 06 · Software Products · Challenging
Security Software Products
India has limited track record in pure-play security software products at global scale. Quick Heal/Seqrite is the clearest exception — a genuine enterprise endpoint security platform built in Pune. The lack of deep-tech security venture capital infrastructure, combined with brain drain to the US, makes global software product leadership harder to achieve than services. The window is opening as Indian tech matures.
Difficult

Final Investment Verdict

The Definitive Answers to the Core Questions

Synthesising the full research framework — 15 sections, 7 value chain tiers, 8 global bottleneck owners, 40 India compounders, and the ASML analysis — into direct, unambiguous investment conclusions. Think like a sovereign wealth fund allocating capital for two decades.

Question 01 · Where does value live?
Identity (Tier 1) and AI Security (Tier 6) are the two highest-conviction long-term positions in the security economy.
Identity has the best combination of existing moat, regulatory floor, and AI-era growth catalyst (NHI). AI Security is the greenfield category with a guaranteed regulatory demand catalyst and zero incumbent advantage — the most asymmetric entry point in the current cycle.
Identity / IAMAI Security / Governance
Question 02 · Strongest moats by layer?
Identity > Endpoint > Zero Trust Network — in that order of structural durability.
Identity has the deepest switching costs and most regulatory mandates. Endpoint has a compounding data flywheel that can't be replicated. Zero Trust network position (Zscaler, Cloudflare) has architectural switching costs but faces more competitive alternatives than the other two.
CyberArkOktaCrowdStrikeZscaler
Question 03 · Which AI-security categories become mandatory?
LLM Firewall, Non-Human Identity Management, and AI Governance Platform — in order of enterprise urgency.
LLM Firewall becomes mandatory the moment an enterprise deploys AI with access to sensitive data. NHI becomes mandatory as agent populations exceed human employees. AI Governance is mandated by the EU AI Act for all high-risk AI operators by 2026 — the most certain regulatory catalyst in the category.
LLM FirewallsNHI ManagementAI Governance
Question 04 · Strongest pricing power globally?
CyberArk (PAM has no alternatives), CrowdStrike (sensor is embedded), and Microsoft (bundle compresses competition).
CyberArk faces zero credible alternatives for mandated PAM compliance — the functional definition of pricing power. CrowdStrike's sensor cannot be replaced without extended coverage gaps. Microsoft's bundling strategy means it sets prices with no competitive reference point for the security features included in existing licences.
CyberArkCrowdStrikeMicrosoft
Question 05 · Which Indian companies are best positioned?
eMudhra, Subex, KPIT Technologies, and Tanla Platforms — each with a structural moat, not just sector exposure.
eMudhra is the only licensed CA in India — a regulatory moat that cannot be replicated by new entrants. Subex has 300+ global operator relationships that are structurally sticky. KPIT is the automotive cybersecurity engineering leader at scale. Tanla holds the TRAI-mandated DLT monopoly for India's entire commercial SMS infrastructure.
eMudhraSubexKPIT TechTanla
Question 06 · Which segments have highest margins?
Identity (80–85% GM), Endpoint (75–80% GM), and Cloud Security (70–78% GM) — all with strong NRR above 110%.
Identity and Endpoint have the structural characteristics that produce outstanding economics: high switching costs drive low churn, platform expansion drives NRR above 115%, and the regulatory floor prevents price competition. AI Security will match these margins once the category achieves platform status in 3–5 years.
Question 07 · Which are future platform winners?
CrowdStrike (endpoint-out), Palo Alto Networks (explicit platformization), and Microsoft (natural bundler at $20B+ scale).
CrowdStrike is expanding from endpoint telemetry into identity, cloud, and SecOps — using its data advantage as the cross-sell engine. Palo Alto's platformization strategy is the most deliberate attempt to become the single-vendor security stack. Microsoft wins by default in every enterprise that refuses to change licences.
CrowdStrikePalo AltoMicrosoft
Question 08 · Which are merely narrative plays?
Generic "AI-powered" security wrappers with no proprietary data, basic password managers, and commodity VPN providers.
The narrative trap is the same across every security category: a compelling story about AI + security without a proprietary data moat, without switching costs, and without a regulatory mandate. The test is simple — would enterprises notice if this vendor disappeared overnight? If the answer is no, it is a narrative, not a bottleneck.

Special Section

The Security Layer of the AI Economy

The AI economy creates entirely new value pools at the intersection of security and AI infrastructure. The largest long-term wealth creation will accrue to platforms that own the trust, identity, verification, and governance layers of the emerging AI-native enterprise. These are the six largest emerging value pools.

🤖
AI Agent Identity Network
Every autonomous AI agent requires an identity, permission set, and credential. The platform that becomes the identity provider for non-human identities at scale — managing millions of agent lifecycles — will be the Visa of the AI economy. Zero current vendor has dominant position. First-mover advantage is decisive.
Estimated 2035 market: $15B+ · CAGR: ~45%
🔐
AI Governance Infrastructure
Every enterprise deploying high-risk AI under the EU AI Act, US federal AI rules, or India's upcoming AI governance framework needs audit trails, explainability tooling, and compliance documentation. This is not optional — it carries criminal liability for executives. The market is growing from zero to multi-billion in 24 months.
EU AI Act compliance window: 2025–2026 · Mandatory
🧠
LLM Security Proxy Layer
Every enterprise AI API call requires inspection, logging, prompt sanitization, and output filtering. The proxy layer sitting between enterprise applications and AI APIs becomes the toll booth for all AI traffic. Cloudflare AI Gateway is the most mature current candidate. The winner processes billions of AI interactions daily at network scale.
Estimated 2035 market: $8B+ · Winner-take-most
👤
Human Verification Trust Layer
As deepfakes proliferate and AI agents dominate digital interactions, proving a counterparty is human becomes a premium security service. Liveness detection, cryptographic human attestation, and digital watermarking become the foundational trust layer of the AI economy — the SSL certificate equivalent for human presence verification.
Trigger: First $1B+ deepfake fraud event · Imminent
⚡
Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration
NIST finalized post-quantum cryptography standards in 2024. Every enterprise must migrate all encryption infrastructure within 5–10 years — a non-negotiable, time-bound technology replacement cycle. "Harvest now, decrypt later" attacks make the urgency greater than the migration deadline suggests. First wave: government and financial services. Second: universal.
$30B+ mandatory replacement cycle · 2025–2032
🌐
ML Supply Chain Security
The AI supply chain — model repositories, fine-tuning datasets, ML libraries, pre-trained checkpoints — is largely unprotected and represents a single point of failure for enterprises deploying foundation models. Analogous to SolarWinds for software supply chains, the first major compromised model supply chain incident will trigger immediate mandatory enterprise adoption of ML supply chain security tooling.
Catalyst: First major AI supply chain attack
