Falcon 9's reusable first stage compressed cost-to-LEO from $54,500/kg to ~$1,500/kg. Starship targets sub-$100/kg at scale — a 600× reduction from the Shuttle baseline. Every downstream business case that previously failed on launch economics is now viable. This is the unlock that makes everything else possible.

Rocket Lab's Electron recovery program and ISRO's NGLV reusability-first architecture represent the next wave. The airline model applied to rocketry is now proven.

Part 02 — The Space Economy Map

Building the Complete Space Value Chain

The space economy is an eight-tier system. Each tier has distinct margin profiles, moat characteristics, and capital intensity. Understanding where value concentrates — and why — is the foundation of every investment decision in this super-cycle.

Tier 01 · Launch Systems
Launch & Propulsion Infrastructure
Market Size 2024E
$20B
→ $70B by 2035E
▲ 12% CAGR
The foundational enabling layer — and increasingly, a contested utility. SpaceX controls ~65% of global commercial launches, with structural cost advantages that incumbents cannot close within a decade. Rocket Lab's Electron dominates dedicated smallsat launch. However, the investment thesis in launch extends beyond rocket operators: the real value concentration is in the propulsion subsystem supplier network. Aerojet Rocketdyne's RL10, Rocket Lab's Rutherford engine architecture, and the emerging electric propulsion ecosystem (Apollo Fusion, Busek, Enpulsion) are qualification-locked with pricing power independent of which launch brand wins market share. Reusability drives volume; subsystems capture margin.

Tier 02 · Spacecraft
Spacecraft & Satellite Systems
Market Size 2024E
$30B
→ $120B by 2035E
▲ 13% CAGR
Satellite buses are commoditizing — York Space, LeoStella, and Exolaunch demonstrate sub-$5M platforms — but critical subsystems are not. Radiation-hardened electronics (BAE Systems' RAD750, Microchip Technology, Renesas space-grade lines), reaction wheel assemblies (Collins Aerospace, Vectronic Aerospace, Honeywell), and high-efficiency solar arrays (SolAero Technologies, now Northrop Grumman; Spectrolab) command 40–70% gross margins from a qualification-locked customer base where re-certification takes 3–5 years. The key insight: as constellation scale increases from tens to thousands of satellites, the economics of volume procurement favor standardized buses — but the subsystems remain custom, qualified, and irreplaceable within a mission's lifecycle.

Ground Infrastructure & Control

Ground infrastructure evolves from fixed mega-stations to cloud-hosted virtual ground networks. AWS Ground Station, Azure Orbital, and KSAT's 20+ station global network monetize "ground-as-a-service" at per-minute pricing — transforming capital assets into operational expenses for constellation operators. As constellations grow to 50,000+ satellites, ground contact capacity becomes a genuine bottleneck. The hyperscaler buildout (AWS, Microsoft, Google) into space ground infrastructure signals that this is being treated as cloud infrastructure extension, not satellite support — with corresponding pricing power.

Tier 04 · Communications
Space-Based Communications
Market Size 2024E
$40B
→ $180B by 2035E
▲ 14% CAGR
The highest-value near-term opportunity in the space economy. LEO broadband is executing the cable company playbook at planetary scale — astronomical capital entry costs create near-permanent first-mover advantages. Starlink's $3B+ ARR growing ~40% YoY validates the model. Critical sub-tier: optical inter-satellite links (ISLs) manufactured by Mynaric (Germany), Tesat-Spacecom (Airbus subsidiary), and CACI International are the connective tissue of every major constellation — a hardware supply chokepoint with multi-year qualification cycles and no near-term competitive alternative. Every constellation operator needs ISL terminals. There are two to three qualified suppliers globally. This is a structural bottleneck.

EO is completing a fundamental transition from imagery sales to intelligence-as-a-service. Planet Labs (300+ satellites, daily global coverage), BlackSky (rapid-revisit tasking at 30-minute cadence), Maxar (50cm resolution, classified contracts), and Satellogic (multispectral at scale) compete not on camera quality but on AI analysis depth and historical archive size. A multi-year daily-global dataset becomes an impossible-to-replicate training corpus for change detection, agricultural monitoring, supply chain intelligence, and military ISR. The data moat deepens annually — each day of archive makes the dataset more defensible. The winner is the one with the best model trained on the most historical Earth data.

Tier 07 · Resources
In-Space Resources & ISRU
Market Size 2024E
~$0B
→ $1B+ by 2035E (ISRU only)
▲ SPECULATIVE / HIGH CONVICTION LONG
The most speculative tier with the longest-horizon payoffs. Near-term viability centers on lunar in-situ resource utilization (ISRU) — extracting water ice from permanently shadowed craters for electrolysis into LH₂/LOX propellant. Intuitive Machines, Astrobotic, and ispace Japan are building the delivery infrastructure; Masten Space (now Astrobotic) developed the ISRU extraction hardware. The U.S. Commercial Space Launch Competitiveness Act (2015) and Artemis Accords create nascent property rights — the legal framework for a resource economy is being constructed in real time. Institutional investors with 15–20 year horizons should monitor this tier closely; the infrastructure being built today (CLPS landers, lunar comms, GPS extension) is the supply chain for a future resource economy.

Space Software & Intelligence Platforms
Market Size 2024E
$4B

▲ 20% CAGR — FASTEST GROWING
The most underappreciated and highest return-on-capital tier in the space economy. As LEO fills with 50,000+ satellites, orbital traffic management transitions from optional to legally regulated — creating a potential de facto software monopoly. Slingshot Aerospace (DoD orbital intelligence), Ansys (space simulation; ~70% gross margins), Cognitive Space (AI mission planning), and Vyoma Space (collision avoidance) are building Palantir-grade operational moats in mission-critical workflows. The winner of orbital traffic management standards becomes the Microsoft of space — a platform upon which every satellite operator depends, every day, under regulatory compulsion. The software margin structure (90%+ gross, low capex, network effects) makes this the best risk/reward tier for public market investors.
