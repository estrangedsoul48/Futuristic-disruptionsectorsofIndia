Part Seven

The Glass Substrate Revolution

Advanced packaging substrates are becoming the critical bottleneck in AI semiconductor infrastructure. The transition from organic to glass core substrates is one of the most consequential materials transitions in semiconductor packaging history — and one of the most time-sensitive investment opportunities in the photonics super-cycle.

7.1 Why Substrates Became the Frontier

As Moore’s Law scaling decelerates, the industry has pivoted to advanced packaging — placing multiple chiplets in extremely close proximity on a single substrate — to achieve the density, bandwidth, and power efficiency that monolithic scaling once provided freely. Technologies such as Intel EMIB, TSMC CoWoS and SoIC, and Samsung X-Cube have made the substrate the central engineering challenge of next-generation AI hardware.

As AI accelerator packages exceed 70×70 mm and target 100×100 mm, substrate engineering requirements become extraordinarily demanding: sub-5 µm line-and-space routing, CTE matching to silicon, extreme flatness for photolithographic processing, and signal integrity above 50 GHz through the substrate structure itself. Organic ABF substrates — the current standard built on glass-fibre-reinforced epoxy resin — face inherent physical limitations that glass substrates solve at the material level.

Organic substrates suffer three critical limitations at AI scale: CTE mismatch with silicon (15–20 ppm/°C vs near-zero for silicon) causes warpage under thermal cycling; achievable routing density is limited by organic dielectric surface roughness; and organic materials have significantly higher dielectric losses at the signal frequencies required by next-generation AI accelerators. Glass substrates address all three simultaneously.

7.2 Intel’s Commitment and the Qualification Window

Intel publicly committed in 2023 to glass substrate deployment by 2026, identifying it as the substrate for its next-generation packaging roadmap beyond EMIB. Intel’s published research documents a glass-core TGV substrate delivering 10× better power delivery performance versus organic equivalents at equivalent via pitch — a critical metric for AI accelerator power integrity.

Intel’s primary glass substrate development partner is Corning, giving Corning first-mover qualification status and early access to engineering specifications. Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek, AGC, and Nippon Electric Glass (NEG) are all in active development. The qualification window is open today and will substantially close by 2026–2027. Companies that achieve qualification in this window will hold positions analogous to Ajinomoto’s ABF resin monopoly in organic substrates — sustained pricing power for a decade or more as switching costs prevent customers from re-qualifying alternative suppliers.

Glass Substrate Investment Verdict

Glass substrates are positioned to become a strategic material with near-term toll-booth characteristics, transitioning to a semi-commoditised advanced material over 10–15 years. Corning, AGC, LG Innotek, and Samsung Electro-Mechanics are the primary global beneficiaries. No Indian company holds a current position in this value pool, but India’s ATMP mission creates a medium-term development opportunity worth tracking from 2027 onward — particularly if STL or an Indian ATMP facility pursues TGV capability development under the Semiconductor Mission.

Part Eight

India’s Photonics Opportunity

India occupies a distinct and evolving position in the global photonics value chain — strong in fiber infrastructure, developing in defence electro-optics, nascent in higher-value silicon photonics. An honest assessment of where India can and cannot compete is the essential starting point for identifying genuine investment opportunity versus narrative exposure.

8.1 Fiber Optics — India’s Strongest Position

India’s most globally competitive photonics position is in optical fiber cable manufacturing. Sterlite Technologies (STL) has built a vertically integrated fiber business with manufacturing in India, Italy, and the UK, and has developed indigenous optical fiber preform manufacturing capability — the most technically demanding step in the supply chain, historically monopolised by Corning, Sumitomo, and Shin-Etsu.

The domestic demand catalyst is structural and durable: BharatNet Phase III commits to fiber connectivity for 600,000 villages; the 5G rollout demands dense optical backhaul; and India’s data-centre boom requires sustained optical cable deployment. This domestic demand base funds technology development and provides the volume that underpins export competitiveness. STL’s indigenous preform capability — if expanded — represents a genuine structural cost advantage for fiber supply to South Asia, Africa, and the Middle East.

8.2 Defence Electro-Optics — A Hidden Strength

India’s defence sector has been a consistent but under-appreciated developer of electro-optical technology. BEL’s Electro-Optics division produces thermal imagers, laser rangefinders, image intensifiers, and optronic payloads. Paras Defence and Space Technologies manufactures large-diameter optics for space imaging, periscopes, and electro-optical sensor assemblies — its 1-metre class space telescope optical system is a genuine technical achievement.

The Atmanirbhar Bharat defence indigenisation policy provides a decade-long structural tailwind. Export controls and national security requirements force indigenous development, and the policy mandate creates a protected domestic market that funds the deep technical capability necessary for future export competitiveness. Defence optics is the highest-moat segment within India’s current photonics ecosystem, and the one most likely to produce global export champions over the next 15 years.

India’s Realistic Competitive Tier Map

Compete today: Optical fiber and cable manufacturing (STL — global scale, preform capability); Defence electro-optic systems (BEL, Paras Defence — protected domestic market); Optical networking equipment (Tejas Networks, TATA Group); Passive fiber components for FTTH rollout; Precision manufacturing for space and defence optics (MTAR, Centum).

Build in 5–10 years: Advanced fiber preform manufacturing; compound semiconductor materials (with Semiconductor Mission investment); optical transceiver assembly (PLI leverage); LiDAR components for industrial/automotive; advanced optical coatings for defence and solar.

Not competing meaningfully now: Semiconductor laser manufacturing (requires III-V fabs absent from India); silicon photonics (requires sub-45nm foundry access); specialty optical glass; coherent optical DSP silicon. These gaps are structural and require 10–15 years of sustained investment to address.

BharatNet + 5G — The Structural Demand Catalyst

BharatNet Phase III and full 5G rollout represent demand for hundreds of millions of fiber-km of optical cable over the next 5–7 years. This domestic demand provides the capital and volume to fund technology capability development — the same foundation that allowed Corning to build global-scale competitive advantage in optical fiber from a position of domestic carrier demand in the 1970s–1980s. The parallel is structurally valid and historically instructive.

Part Eleven

Future Photonics Toll Booths

Identifying the companies globally and in India positioned to become the ASML, Synopsys, TSMC, and Corning of the photonics era — durable structural gatekeepers who will collect economic rent as the light-based technology revolution scales over the coming two decades.

Corning Incorporated
Incumbent toll booth of glass and fiber
The Corning of Photonics (Literally)
Already the dominant global toll booth in optical materials. The AI-driven glass substrate opportunity represents a second wave of Corning-style monopoly formation — this time at the semiconductor packaging layer rather than the telecommunications layer. Corning has already run this playbook once with optical fiber in the 1970s–1990s and is positioned to replicate it with glass substrates in the 2020s–2030s. Dual compounding from an existing toll booth (optical fiber) plus an emerging one (glass substrates) is an exceptionally rare combination.
Why It Becomes a Toll Booth
170 years of glass science process know-how — cannot be replicated through capital alone
First-mover Intel qualification in glass substrates creates an industry reference standard
Optical fiber pricing power demonstrated; glass substrate analog structurally similar
Dual exposure: optical fiber (existing toll booth) + glass substrates (emerging toll booth)
Synopsys / Ansys — PDA Platform
Software toll booth of photonic design
Synopsys of Photonics
Photonic design automation is today where EDA was in 1985 — critical, fragmented, and awaiting consolidation around a dominant platform. The company that establishes the standard PDA workflow for silicon photonics and glass substrate electromagnetic simulation will command 75–90% gross margins and extreme switching costs. The EDA analogy is instructive: Synopsys and Cadence now earn $10B+ annually from tools once considered a niche research utility. Once a design team adopts a PDA workflow and a photonic foundry publishes its process design kit (PDK) for that tool, switching cost is 2–3 years of re-training and re-validation.
Why It Becomes a Toll Booth
PDK lock-in: photonic foundries publish PDKs for qualified tools only — creates ecosystem dependency
Software economics: near-zero marginal cost, perpetual seat licensing, expanding design complexity
AI-assisted photonic design (generative PIC design) will concentrate further around the leading platform
Broadcom (Silicon Photonics ASICs)
The merchant silicon standard-setter
Nvidia of Optical AI Infrastructure
Broadcom’s dominance in AI switching silicon (Tomahawk, Trident) combined with its expanding co-packaged optics programme positions it as the most integrated supplier of compute-plus-optical infrastructure for AI data centres. Custom chip programmes (Google TPU, Meta MTIA) embed optical interfaces at the silicon architecture level, creating dependencies that persist across 3–5 year chip generations. The toll booth emerges when the optical interface becomes inseparable from the switching ASIC — at which point Broadcom’s silicon defines the optical standard for the industry.
Why It Becomes a Toll Booth
Switching silicon qualification at hyperscale is 18–36 months — sticky revenues across chip generations
CPO integration makes optical interface inseparable from switch ASIC — architectural lock-in
Custom AI chip programmes embed optical co-design at the earliest specification stage
Lumentum + Coherent (Duopoly)
Toll booth of semiconductor laser sources
ASML of Laser Sources
The DFB laser duopoly between Lumentum and Coherent in coherent optical communications is a structural toll booth that has persisted for over 15 years. Every coherent optical transceiver shipped worldwide contains a DFB laser from one of these two companies. The qualification cycle — 5–7 years for a new laser supplier at a major optical networking OEM — makes this duopoly self-reinforcing. Any new entrant faces years of uncompensated qualification work before generating revenue, making the investment case unattractive for the capital required. AI data-centre optical demand is growing faster than either company’s capacity expansion rate.
Why It Remains a Toll Booth
III-V compound semiconductor epi growth — 20+ years of yield optimisation not transferable via licensing
Multi-year qualification at Ciena, Infinera, Nokia, Fujitsu — new entrants face the same clock
Demand for DFB lasers from AI cluster optics is growing faster than capacity can scale
Hamamatsu Photonics
Toll booth of scientific photon detection
The Silent ASML
Hamamatsu’s photomultiplier tube monopoly is the most silent but most profound toll booth in the photonics ecosystem. Its components are embedded in particle physics experiments, medical imaging, semiconductor wafer inspection tools, and ASML’s own EUV light source — making it one of the few companies whose absence would simultaneously impact multiple multi-trillion-dollar industries. The PMT is the enabling component that almost no investor discusses, and that almost no customer could replace.
Why It Remains a Toll Booth
PMT photocathode and dynode know-how: 60 years of unchallenged process expertise
CERN, medical PET, KLA inspection: qualification requires 5–10 years; no commercial alternative
SiPM (solid-state replacement) business also led by Hamamatsu — cannibalising itself intentionally and maintaining leadership in the successor technology
STL (India) — Regional Candidate
India’s most credible toll booth candidate
India’s Corning Candidate
STL is India’s most credible candidate for a regional photonics toll booth. Its preform manufacturing capability — if expanded and deepened — creates a structural cost and supply security advantage for fiber supply to South Asia, Africa, and the Middle East that international competitors cannot overcome on cost or logistics. The path to toll-booth status requires STL to expand preform capacity, qualify additional specialty fiber types, and build direct Tier-1 global carrier relationships that Corning has built over decades. This is a 10–15 year compounding story, not a 3-year trade.
What STL Must Execute
Expand preform capacity to achieve cost leadership versus imported preform at commercial volumes
Qualify specialty fiber types demanded by AI infrastructure (ultra-low-loss SMF, bend-insensitive)
Build direct Tier-1 carrier relationships in US, Europe, and SE Asia beyond current OEM channels
Part Thirteen

Capital Cycle Analysis

Understanding where capital is flooding, where it remains scarce, which bottlenecks will persist through cycle pressure, and which technologies face commoditisation — the analytical discipline for separating structural winners from cyclical plays and narrative traps.

★ Structural Winners — Capital Scarce, Demand Durable
Specialty optical glass and preform manufacturing — capital cannot replicate the process know-how; returns will be sustained across cycles.
Semiconductor laser (DFB, VCSEL) manufacturing — III-V capacity expands slowly; qualification creates demand floors. Lumentum and Coherent benefit structurally for the decade.
Photonic design automation software — essentially no capital intensity; market remains underinvested relative to eventual opportunity. Winning platform compounds for decades.
Glass substrate technology — qualification-gated bottleneck; new capital cannot enter without multi-year customer approval. Corning and AGC benefit with protected margins.
India defence electro-optics — policy-protected, long-cycle, underinvested. Paras Defence, BEL, Data Patterns benefit for a decade through mandate-driven indigenisation.
◆ Cyclical Winners — Capital Flooding, Returns Temporarily High
Optical fiber cable manufacturing — BharatNet and 5G cycles drive exceptional demand today. Returns are good but eventual normalisation expected as capacity builds globally post-2027.
Co-packaged optics components (near-term) — AI capex surge driving exceptional CPO demand. Supply ramp will follow capital; returns normalise over 3–5 years as multiple CPO suppliers qualify.
Data-centre optical interconnects (pluggable) — AI cluster build-out driving a surge in 400G/800G transceiver demand. Cyclical in character; track capital entry carefully as Chinese competitors scale.
Indian fiber passive components — BharatNet surge creates excellent near-term demand; normalises post-rollout completion circa 2028–2030. Position for the cycle, not permanently.
↓ Commodity Traps — Capital Flooding, Differentiation Absent
Pluggable transceiver module assembly — low-margin assembly facing CPO structural headwind. Chinese manufacturers compressing margins. Avoid without a differentiated technology story.
Standard optical fiber cable (non-specialty) — commodity pricing as global capacity expands. Fiberhome, ZTT, and other Chinese manufacturers compete aggressively on price in global tenders.
Generic LiDAR sensor manufacturing — significant capital entered on autonomous vehicle optimism; most entrants lack differentiated technology and face Chinese cost competition. High attrition expected.
Standard optical passive components (commodity) — price deflation driven by automation and Asian manufacturing scale. No long-term pricing power for undifferentiated connector and splice manufacturing.
⚠ Narrative Traps — Attractive Story, Unclear Economics
Optical computing (all-optical logic) — compelling physics but unclear commercialisation path for general-purpose computing. Remain watchful; do not pre-position at premium valuations for a decade-away application.
Generic AI photonics plays — many companies claim photonics AI exposure without genuine revenue content. Verify photonics revenue as a percentage of total before establishing any position.
Indian silicon photonics ambitions (pre-revenue) — exciting narratives; operational revenue is minimal. Track and monitor; do not invest today at premium valuations ahead of proven manufacturing capability.
Solid-state LiDAR for consumer automotive (most entrants) — only 2–3 global players will capture volume economics in automotive LiDAR. Assume most announced entrants will not survive to volume production.

The capital cycle teaches a singular lesson in photonics: the segments that attract the least capital today — specialty optical materials, photonic design software, compound semiconductor laser sources — are precisely the segments where the most durable returns will be generated. The segments attracting the most capital — fiber cable, pluggable transceivers, consumer LiDAR — are where returns will normalise most rapidly. Position accordingly.

AI, Data Centres &
The Photonic Future

The convergence of AI compute scaling, data-centre power constraints, and optical physics fundamentals is creating a once-in-a-generation infrastructure transformation. This section maps the specific value pools at the intersection of AI and photonics over the 2025–2035 decade.

The Data Centre of 2030 — An Optical Fabric

The data centre of 2030 will be architecturally distinct from that of 2024 in one fundamental dimension: the dominant interconnect medium within racks, between racks, and between data-centre halls will be optical rather than electrical. This shift is driven not by a single technology breakthrough but by the simultaneous convergence of five forcing functions: AI compute scaling beyond electrical bandwidth limits; CPO deployment reducing pluggable transceiver power and cost; optical switching maturity enabling nanosecond-latency photonic network fabrics; silicon photonics cost reduction through CMOS volume; and glass substrate packaging enabling tighter optical-electrical co-integration at the die level.

The value pools emerging from this architectural transition include: optical switching silicon (enabling photonic packet routing without electrical conversion — Ayar Labs, Lightmatter, and eventually Intel and Broadcom with integrated photonic switches); co-packaged optical engines (integrated into switch and accelerator packages — Marvell, Broadcom, and Coherent’s silicon photonics programmes compete here); and optical memory interconnects (connecting DRAM arrays to compute via optical links — still in early R&D but potentially the largest single value pool if CXL photonic extensions achieve broad adoption after 2028).

For India specifically, the most near-term and concrete demand driver is the optical fiber cabling and DWDM infrastructure within India’s rapidly expanding data-centre campuses. Every AI data-centre campus — and India is building dozens — requires tens of thousands of fiber-km of intra-campus optical connectivity. This domestic data-centre optical demand is the most underappreciated near-term demand driver for Indian photonics companies and justifies sustained attention to STL, HFCL, and fiber passive component suppliers through 2030.

Part Fifteen — Final Investment Verdict

The Investment Verdict

Fifteen sections of analysis converge on a clear set of conclusions about where value lives, which bottlenecks matter most, and which companies — global and Indian — are best positioned to compound over the 2025–2045 investment horizon.

📍
Where Does Value Concentrate?
Materials → Software → Laser Sources
The greatest concentration of long-term economic value in photonics is in: (1) specialty optical materials where process know-how creates non-replicable moats; (2) photonic design automation software where switching costs compound indefinitely; and (3) semiconductor laser sources where III-V compound barriers sustain duopoly pricing. These three layers capture the highest gross margins, highest ROIC, and strongest pricing power in the entire value chain.
🔒
Which Bottlenecks Matter Most?
Laser Sources Now. Glass Substrates Urgent.
DFB laser sources (Lumentum/Coherent duopoly) are the most durable existing bottleneck. Glass substrates are the most time-sensitive emerging bottleneck — the qualification window closes within 24–36 months. Photonic design software is the most undervalued future bottleneck. All three deserve priority attention. Pluggable transceiver assembly deserves active avoidance as CPO erodes its structural market position.
🏆
Highest Margins, Strongest Moats
Hamamatsu, Corning, Lumentum, Coherent
These four demonstrate the highest combination of gross margin, ROIC, and competitive moat durability in the global photonics ecosystem. Hamamatsu’s near-monopoly in scientific photon detection, Corning’s materials science breadth, and the Lumentum/Coherent laser source duopoly each represent 15–20+ year compounding positions that require no architectural thesis bet — they win regardless of which AI chip architecture prevails.
🇮🇳
Best Positioned Indian Companies
STL, Paras Defence, Data Patterns, BEL
STL is India’s highest-quality photonics compounder with genuine global competitive capability and a preform moat in development. Paras Defence is the highest-technology-intensity small-cap with genuine optical manufacturing depth. Data Patterns compounds on defence optics with exceptional margins. BEL provides the most durable revenue visibility through near-monopoly defence electronics positions across a decade of assured indigenisation demand.
💎
Most Underappreciated Small-Caps
Kaveri Telecom, Nibe Ltd, KDDL, Paras Defence
Kaveri Telecom Products is the archetypal hidden supplier — fiber passive components with near-zero analyst coverage and direct BharatNet/Jio Fiber demand exposure. Nibe Ltd’s optoelectronics for defence is another under-covered direct photonics beneficiary. KDDL’s precision machining capability is a credible bridge into defence optical component manufacturing. Paras Defence still trades below the valuation its technical moat and defence optical monopoly should command.
⚠️
Narrative Plays to Avoid
Commodity Transceivers, Generic LiDAR, Pre-Rev. Optical Computing
Optical computing for general-purpose computation, consumer-grade LiDAR companies without a volume automotive design win, and pluggable transceiver assembly businesses facing CPO-driven structural headwinds are the primary narrative traps. Their stories are compelling; their long-term economics are not durable. Separate the valid technological theme of photonics from the commercial viability of individual companies claiming exposure to it.
The Single Most Important Investment Insight

The photonics transition will create enormous wealth — but that wealth will concentrate in the narrow layer of bottleneck owners whose positions cannot be replicated regardless of how much capital floods into the sector. Identify the ASML equivalents — those whose absence would break supply chains — and build patient, long-duration positions in them. Everything else is a trade. The ASML equivalents of photonics are: Corning (materials), Hamamatsu (detection), Lumentum/Coherent (laser sources), and the future dominant photonic design automation platform. In India, STL and Paras Defence are the earliest-stage credible analogues to this tier. Own them before institutional consensus arrives.
